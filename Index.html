Overview

  • Led the mechanical design, manufacturing, testing, and integration of a hydraulic braking system for McGill Baja Racing team.
  • Using Siemens NX, designed a full assembly model of the brake system, with relevant calipers, master cylinders, and rotors outlined below.

Methodology

  • Delivered zero hydraulic failures across dynamic testing and multi-hour endurance race conditions.

Overview

  • Authored a technical evaluation paper for WCOM 206 (Communication in Engineering) that assesses industrial carbon capture and storage methods that mitigate CO₂ emissions.
  • Evaluated three distinct capture methods - Oxyfuel Combustion, Underground Coal Gasification (UCG), and Direct Air Capture (DAC) - to identify the most practical technology for large-scale industrial application.

Methodology & Evaluation Criteria

  • Evaluated technologies against three core metrics: Economic feasibility, system efficiency, and environmental impact.
  • Performed a comparative analysis of each technology using data from peer-reviewed journals and industry reports to assess their performance and viability.

Results & Impact

  • Oxyfuel combustion represents the most balanced near-term solution for large-scale industrial applications due to high capture efficiency (90%) and low operational capture costs ($35/tCO₂), along with proven widespread usage in existing power plants.
  • UCG is a strong alternative for coal mining, offering strong cost efficiency and lower environmental impact, while DAC holds the greatest long-term potential once cost and efficiency problems are resolved.
